# Maple — four-minute HABSIDA pitch

Prepared September 25, 2026. Working brief from the supplied Demo Day image: four-minute pitch, four-minute Q&A, presentation display plus optional demo TV. Rehearse to 3:50. The times below are rehearsal checkpoints, not a guarantee based on word count.

**Core idea:** Help the next hackathon happen.

**Product description:** Maple is a sponsorship marketplace connecting event organizers with sponsors interested in their audience.

The proposed spoken copy below paraphrases the participant's recollection of the opening. It does not quote the organizer verbatim. Demonstrate only working actions that the team has tested, and label sample event details and sponsor accounts. No pilot or sponsorship agreement is implied.

## 1. Product and shared interest — 0:00–0:20

**On screen:** Maple / Help the next hackathon happen / A marketplace for event sponsorship.

> We're Maple. We help event organizers and sponsors find each other.
>
> Quick show of hands: who would like another hackathon like this?
>
> That's the future we want to help support.

**Delivery:** Raise your own hand. Pause for two or three seconds, lower it, and continue. If nobody responds, the same next sentence still works. Do not wait for applause or comment on the response. Allow about eight seconds for the question and pause within this opening's twenty seconds.

## 2. Origin and problem — 0:20–0:50

**On screen:** This hackathon gave us our starting point. Smaller text: Sponsorship was difficult. The ambition: two hackathons a year. Label: Paraphrase of opening remarks, as recalled by our team.

> At the opening, we heard how difficult finding sponsors had been, and how the organizers contributed their own resources to make this event happen.
>
> They want to run two hackathons a year.
>
> That became our starting point: could we make finding support for the next one easier?

**Delivery:** Speak warmly and slow down on “two hackathons a year.” The organizers' effort deserves appreciation. Avoid implying that Maple has already solved their problem.

**Optional Florian reference:** Replace the last sentence, rather than adding time: “The organizer also mentioned Florian's contribution. We wanted to make finding that kind of support easier.” Acknowledge him briefly, then address the whole room. Collective recognition is equally appropriate; naming a judge is optional.

## 3. The exchange — 0:50–1:15

**On screen:** Audience + Support needed + Sponsor benefit. Below: Post → Propose → Discuss.

> Organizers need money, venues, food, or tools. Sponsors need relevant audiences and clear benefits.
>
> Maple brings those needs together. Organizers publish their event and sponsorship offer. Sponsors can publish what they want to support. Both sides can assess the fit before starting a conversation.

**Delivery:** Give “sponsors need” equal emphasis. This is a business exchange: possible benefits include meeting developers, receiving product feedback, or reaching potential hires. Those are examples, not promises of this event's outcomes.

## 4. Demonstration — 1:15–2:10

**On presentation display:** Imagine preparing the next HABSIDA hackathon. Event → Proposal → Conversation.

**On TV:** One rehearsed product journey, visibly marked “Demo — illustrative event details and sponsor account.” No invented confirmed date or announced Winter edition.

> Let me show you what preparing the next HABSIDA hackathon could look like. These package details and the sponsor account are examples.
>
> Here is the audience, the support needed, and what a sponsor would receive.
>
> A sponsor can review the fit and send a specific proposal.
>
> The organizer receives it, and both sides can discuss the offer with the event details attached.
>
> That's the step we're making possible: a relevant sponsorship conversation.

**Operator sequence:** 12 seconds on the event; 8 on relevant criteria or search; 15 on a prepared proposal; 15 on the received proposal and conversation; 5 on the outcome. Use controlled test accounts and preloaded sessions. Submission and receipt must work in rehearsal. Otherwise, present a clearly identified prototype recording or screenshots, subject to event rules.

**Display cue:** Say “On the TV” once if the layout needs it. A teammate operates while the speaker faces the room. Do not show registration, type a long message, tour settings, or mark a real event completed.

**Failure line:** “I'll show the prepared capture of the same prototype journey.” Switch promptly. If using static images, say “screenshots,” not “live demo.”

## 5. What we know — 2:10–2:35

**On screen:** Observed: sponsorship difficulty. Built: prototype workflow. Next test: relevant conversations.

> We have a concrete problem example and a prototype. We still need to prove that organizers and sponsors will use it repeatedly.
>
> Our next test is whether real users start relevant conversations and agree a sponsorship. Interest in the idea alone is not enough.

**Evidence rule:** Replace this with stronger results only when documented. An example listing, opening anecdote, raised hand, and real sponsorship agreement are distinct evidence. The source review did not verify the complete deployed workflow; the team must rehearse it.

## 6. First users and growth — 2:35–3:05

**On screen:** Start locally → recruit relevant sponsors → curate events → expand after useful matches. Suggested first segment: English-speaking tech events in Seoul/Incheon.

> We propose starting with tech events and hackathons in this community. We'll recruit sponsors directly, learn what they want to support, and help make the first introductions.
>
> Sponsorship platforms already exist. Our bet is useful local matches and current needs from both sides. We'll expand only after that works.

**Status:** This geographic focus is a recommendation based on current access, not a confirmed business decision. In Q&A, name SponsorMyEvent and SponsorPitch honestly. Local focus is a hypothesis to test, not an established competitive advantage.

## 7. Revenue hypothesis — 3:05–3:25

**On screen:** Today: free early access. Next: test paid organizer tools and optional visibility.

> Maple is free during early access. Once users receive value, we plan to test paid organizer tools and optional visibility.
>
> We haven't validated prices yet. Sponsorship payments are currently arranged directly between the organizations.

**Delivery:** No invented revenue or commission. Be ready to explain why an organizer running two events annually might prefer a different billing model from a monthly subscription.

## 8. Return to the next event — 3:25–3:50

**On screen:** Help the next hackathon happen. Proposed next step: explore a HABSIDA pilot. Team names, one true qualification, contact or tested QR code.

> This hackathon gave us a place to build. Its sponsorship challenge gave us a problem to work on.
>
> We'd like to explore a pilot for the next hackathon with the organizers after today.
>
> We're Maple. Help the next hackathon happen.

**Delivery:** Pause briefly before the final sentence. Leave the slide visible for Q&A. Do not ask the organizers or Florian to commit in front of the room. The pilot request invites a later conversation. A verified team credential belongs on the slide; add it verbally only if a timed rehearsal leaves room.

## Four-minute Q&A preparation

Give a direct initial answer in roughly 15–30 seconds, then pause for follow-up. These are rehearsal examples, not claims of results.

| Likely question | Suggested answer |
|---|---|
| Was the problem finding sponsors, or a lack of budget? | “We don't know yet. The opening gave us a problem to investigate. Maple can help surface relevant opportunities and structure the discussion. It cannot create a budget. We'll test fit, timing, and sponsor interest in the pilot.” |
| Is HABSIDA your customer? | “Its experience inspired the idea, and we use it as an example. We're proposing a pilot conversation; we are not announcing a signed customer agreement.” Adjust only if an actual agreement exists. |
| Is one organizer enough to validate a startup? | “It gives us a starting point. We need evidence from other organizers and sponsors, real proposals, and repeat use before calling demand validated.” |
| Why would a sponsor join? | “To find relevant events with clear audiences, needs, and benefits. We need to learn each sponsor's criteria and prove the opportunities are useful. More listings alone do not provide that value.” |
| Why wouldn't people use personal contacts or existing marketplaces? | “They may, and those are real alternatives. We need to show that current sponsor needs, structured event information, and a focused local community produce better matches or less work.” |
| How do you get the first sponsors? | “Direct outreach to a small group with current interest, then carefully selected event opportunities and assisted introductions. We will measure real replies, not just account signups.” |
| Why pay a monthly fee for two events a year? | “That is a real pricing question. Monthly pricing is unvalidated. We need to test when organizers receive value and whether per-event or periodic payment makes more sense.” |
| What stops users leaving after an introduction? | “Reusable event details and proposal history may help with the next event, but repeat use is unproven. We need to measure it rather than assume the first introduction creates retention.” |
| How would a HABSIDA pilot work? | “If the organizers are interested, we would first understand the next event's actual needs, recruit sponsors with relevant criteria, and measure substantive two-way conversations and any resulting agreements. The scope would be agreed together.” |

## Interaction and rehearsal choices

- Use one audience question. A second vote, phone signup, or live judge role-play creates another timing dependency. Save product exploration for afterward.
- Make the slide itself part of the story: start with “the next hackathon,” show it taking shape in the demo, and return to the same phrase at the close.
- A genuine photo of this event can support the opening if you have a suitable one to use. A simple title works without it. Do not invent attendee testimonials or present stock imagery as this event.
- Avoid jokes about the organizers' finances, “we will save HABSIDA,” or “Florian, will you sponsor us?” The emotional content comes from a shared event and a practical next step.
- At position 17, begin immediately with the product and participation cue. Do not apologize for the late slot or tell the room it must be tired. No evidence here predicts a scoring advantage or disadvantage from pitch order.
- Rehearse aloud with clicks and pauses. If a run exceeds 3:50, cut optional detail; do not speed through the close. Run once with a quiet audience and once with a demo failure.
- If behind at 2:35, shorten slide 6 to: “We'll begin with local tech events, recruit sponsors directly, and curate the first matches. Then we'll test whether users return.” Keep revenue and the closing request.
- Export a PDF containing readable demo stills as a fallback. Test the TV, presentation screen, and final contact link. A PDF alone may not preserve video or animation.
